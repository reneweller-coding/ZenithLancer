# Episode 4 "Greencrown" (Sylvara): intro, ending, briefings, debriefs, radio lines and log crystals.

intro e4_intro
  title "Greencrown"
  from "Sylvara, moon of Vael"
> Above the rings of the gas giant Vael, Sylvara rises: a moon that looks from above like a single green pelt. Its trees stand two hundred metres tall, its rivers glow from within at night, and anyone who watches long enough notices that the lights in the forest do not flicker at random.
>
> Three weeks ago the Tessellate landed here. For the first time in its history, it is losing. The forest is digesting its lattice. Something in the moss and the roots answers the swarm's song with a song of its own – the settlers of New Maren called it the Choir.
>
> In orbit hangs the fleet of the Meridian Directorate, white and navy blue, in “quarantine position”. Admiral Dacian Voss declares that he is protecting the moon. His burn crews are clearing the forest, strip by strip.
>
> The Magpie sinks through the clouds. Quill is cursing the humidity, Dr. Okonjo carries the Root Code from the Shatter in her pocket and hasn't said a word in hours, and ORRIN reports a pattern of light the size of a valley, right in the middle of the canopy. It shows a silver bird with two engines.
>
> “Nadia,” says ORRIN, “I believe we have been invited.”

ending e4_ending
  title "Amber Fades"
  from "Orbit above Sylvara"
> The Dictate breaks into three pieces. Her bow tumbles into the rings of Vael, her stern burns up above the forest, and the Amber Heart shatters in a light they say was seen as far away as Thalassa. In the same instant the swarm's lattice turns violet again. All across the orbit its ships stop short, as if they had lost the thread.
>
> An escape pod breaks away from the wreck and vanishes sunward. No one goes after it.
>
> Then the Curator speaks up, polite as ever. He thanks Pilot Vey for removing a “disturbance”. He regrets the inconvenience. And he announces that the Crown around the sun is ninety-one percent complete.
>
> Down on Sylvara the forest glows. The Choir shows Nadia one last image: a seed falling into an open hand. Later Okonjo puts it under the microscope and sits in front of it, silent, for a long time. “It has learned to digest the lattice,” she says at last. “Not to destroy it. To let go. Perhaps the swarm can be taught that.”
>
> Quill works out the cost of the repairs and crosses the figure out again. ORRIN plots a course for the sun.
>
> It is noon in the Solenne system. It is going to get hot.

  from "Dr. Amara Okonjo"
> Sample 14, taken from the spore cloud over Green-Seven.
>
> The spore docks onto one of the Tessellate's lattice cells and does something I have not seen in twenty years of research: it asks. It reads the cell's building instructions, link by link, and takes it apart in reverse. No fire, no acid. The cell falls apart into the materials it was made from, and the forest absorbs them.
>
> I wrote those building instructions. It took me twelve years to make them indestructible. It took a fungus three weeks to understand them.
>
> I don't know whether I should feel humiliated or comforted. Probably both. Tomorrow I'll take a sample from the river.
