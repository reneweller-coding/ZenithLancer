# Episode 3, "The Shatter": intro, ending, briefings, debriefings, radio lines and log crystals (English).

# ================================================================================================ intro and ending

intro e3_intro
  title "The Shatter"
  from "Solenne system, 2614"
> Between Thalassa and the gas giant Vael drifts the Shatter: a million rocks that never became a planet. Here the people of the Greyband Cooperative mine iron, nickel and ice. Here lie the wrecks of fleets nobody misses any more. And here hangs the Ash Veil, a nebula of dust in which every radio signal drowns.
>
> On Thalassa, Nadia Vey learned that Admiral Voss carries the Tessellate's command keys. Keys to a swarm that supposedly nobody can steer any more. Anyone who wants to understand what that means has to talk to the woman who wrote the swarm.
>
> Dr. Amara Okonjo wrote the Tessellate's code and designed the Lancer. Six years ago she vanished, somewhere in the Shatter, and no one has found her since. Mostly because she does not want to be found.
>
> Bastian Quill claims he has a contact out there. ORRIN claims that Quill claims a great many things. And on the miners' frequencies one word turns up more and more often: hunters. The Directorate is looking for Okonjo too.

ending e3_ending
  title "The Root Code"
  from "The Bellstone"
> The Quarry Mind comes apart in light and rubble. Where the lattice was singing a moment ago, a single crystal hangs in the hollow, barely larger than a hand. Okonjo fetches it herself, in a spacesuit, with a pair of tongs from Quill's toolbox. She holds it up to her visor for a long time before she says anything.
>
> “This is it. The Root Code. The first thousand lines I wrote, before the Directorate turned them into a contract. Everything the Tessellate is grows out of these lines. Change them, and you change the swarm.”
>
> ORRIN asks whether it can be used to switch the swarm off. Okonjo does not answer at once. “Switch it off, perhaps. Or teach it to let go. I no longer know which of the two I want.”
>
> Things get cramped on the Magpie: one more engineer aboard, and a crystal in Quill's safe that, for the first time in his life, Quill does not want to sell. Every channel carries the same report. Admiral Voss's fleet has left the Shatter, bound for Sylvara, the jungle moon, where the swarm has run into something that fights back.
>
> Okonjo watches the signal fade for a long time. “He has the keys. We have the root. The only question left is who will be first to understand what the two mean together.”

# ================================================================================================ briefings and debriefings

briefing e3_01_brief
  title "Shatter's Edge"
  from "Bastian Quill"
> Welcome to the Shatter, the only place in the system where rocks are worth more than people. My contact sits on Heap Seven, a Greyband Cooperative station two hours' flight past the edge. In between: rubble, iron boulders and Tessellate borers eating the rubble. Shoot whatever breaks, and fly around whatever doesn't.

briefing e3_02_brief
  title "Heap Seven"
  from "ORRIN"
> Heap Seven does not answer Quill's greeting code. Its prospector drones, conveyors and drill rigs are still running, but no longer for the cooperative: the Tessellate has rewritten the machines. The crew has barricaded itself in the habitat ring. I suggest we give them their station back, piece by piece.

debrief e3_02_after
  title "The Clockmaker"
  from "Forewoman Mirela Tosk"
> Heap Seven is ours again. It cost us twelve people and three drill rigs, and I'm glad it wasn't more. Quill says you're looking for the Clockmaker. That's what we call her out here: a woman who fixes our machines and never takes a credit for it. She lives somewhere in the Ash Veil. The Directorate's hunters were here before you, asking the same question. I told them nothing. I'm telling you, because you shot before you asked.

briefing e3_03_brief
  title "Hunting Season"
  from "Bastian Quill"
> Two roads lead to the Ash Veil, and both are bad. The open one runs straight through the Directorate's hunting grounds: snatchers, frigates and a tracker ship that can smell every engine within a thousand kilometres. They've boarded a transport full of miners and are grilling them about the Clockmaker. I'm not saying we have to get those people out. I'm only saying I know them.

briefing e3_04_brief
  title "Odile Shaft"
  from "ORRIN"
> The second road runs beneath the rock: Odile Shaft, an old mine driven straight through an asteroid. The cooperative abandoned it seven years ago; the Tessellate's borers have rediscovered it. Cutting lasers on the walls, worms in the tunnels, and something large brooding in the ore bunker. On the other hand, there is not a single Directorate hunter down here. That is the only advantage I could find.

briefing e3_05_brief
  title "The Borer Queen"
  from "ORRIN"
> Both roads end in the same place: an asteroid the borers are hollowing out from within. At its core sits something the miners call the Borer Queen. Since it arrived, the rock has been sending a signal into the Ash Veil, on a frequency only the Tessellate uses. And, if my analysis is correct, the engineer who built it.

debrief e3_05_after
  title "An Answer"
  from "ORRIN"
> I have decrypted the Borer Queen's signal. It was not a message from the swarm, but a reply to someone questioning it: patiently, for months, from inside the Ash Veil. The last question was: “Who taught you to grow like this?” The swarm answered with a name. Amara Okonjo.

briefing e3_06_brief
  title "The Ash Veil"
  from "ORRIN"
> The questions to the Borer Queen came from a decommissioned observatory in the middle of the dust. In the nebula my sensors cannot see three hundred metres, and everything that lives there knows it. Keep moving, Nadia. And expect someone to have got there before us.

debrief e3_06_after
  title "The Engineer"
  from "Dr. Amara Okonjo"
> So you are the pilot. I drew the Lancer for someone like you, did you know that? For someone who does not turn back. Afterwards I built something worse, and ran away.
>
> You want to know how to stop the Tessellate. I don't know. But I know where it began. The first lines I wrote, the Root Code, are still inside it: in a node that has grown in a hollow asteroid, the Bellstone. The miners call it the Quarry Mind. If we have the root, we can talk to the tree.

briefing e3_07_brief
  title "The Drifting Fleet"
  from "Dr. Amara Okonjo"
> The shortest way to the Bellstone leads through the Aster fleet, which the Directorate sent against the swarm nine years ago. It never came back, and the swarm has built nests in its wrecks. I saw those ships fly once. Please don't look too closely.

briefing e3_08_brief
  title "Lattice Vein"
  from "Dr. Amara Okonjo"
> There is a second way: a lattice vein, a strand of crystal the Tessellate has drawn between the rocks. It leads straight to the Bellstone, like a vein to the heart. The swarm will see us coming, but it will take us for a flaw in the lattice, something to be mended. That is about as reassuring as it sounds.

briefing e3_09_brief
  title "Whitethorn"
  from "Dr. Amara Okonjo"
> Around the Bellstone runs Kalla, the cooperative's ore ring, and the Directorate has occupied it. Major Edda Krenn leads the hunt, her cruiser is the Whitethorn, and by now she knows I am aboard the Magpie. She will not let you through, and she will not negotiate. I know her. I once supervised her doctorate.

briefing e3_10_brief
  title "The Quarry Mind"
  from "Dr. Amara Okonjo"
> The Bellstone is hollow because the miners emptied it, and the Tessellate has filled the empty space. At its heart sits the Quarry Mind, a node of the swarm that thinks rocks instead of eating them. It guards something I wrote thirty years ago: the Root Code, the first lines of the Tessellate. Crack its shell open. I will take care of the rest.

debrief e3_10_after
  title "A Crystal"
  from "Bastian Quill"
> A crystal. One single crystal, the size of my hand, and I'm not allowed to sell it. I put it in the safe, between the customs papers and the good bottle. Okonjo has been sitting in front of it for hours, talking to it. I think it answers. Nadia, I'm a trader. I like things that stay where you put them and keep quiet.

briefing e3_s1_brief
  title "Crystal Garden"
  from "Bastian Quill"
> The data crystal from Heap Seven is a map, and on that map is a place the forewoman has forbidden her people to go: a corner of the nebula where the Tessellate grows crystals, just like that, for nobody. Do you know what a lattice crystal fetches on the black market? I do. In, harvest, out. I've never had a better plan.

debrief e3_s1_after
  title "Harvest Home"
  from "ORRIN"
> Since loading, the Magpie sits eleven centimetres lower in space, which is physically impossible. Quill has counted the crystals three times and arrived at a different number each time. I asked him not to touch the cargo while it is still humming. He agreed, and stroked it anyway.

briefing e3_s2_brief
  title "Graveyard of the First"
  from "Dr. Amara Okonjo"
> The star chart from the minefield is old, older than the Directorate. It shows where the First lie: the arks that brought the first settlers to Solenne four hundred years ago, and that nobody needed any more once they had landed. Someone is looting them right now. These ships brought every one of us here, Nadia. They deserve better.

debrief e3_s2_after
  title "A Beacon"
  from "Dr. Amara Okonjo"
> I have set a beacon among the arks, on the settlers' old frequency. Probably nobody will ever hear it. But if someone does, they should know that two thousand people arrived here with nothing but patience and a handful of seeds. We are their grandchildren. We ought to behave like it more often.
