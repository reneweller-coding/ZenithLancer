# Episode 5 (Noonfire): the assault on the Crown, Zenith Station retaken, Voss's last stand, the Curator in the
# sun's corona, and the end of the game. Okonjo's Root Code has two branches: HALT and DONE (German FERTIG); the
# swarm learns the word "done".

intro e5_intro
  title "Noonfire"
  from "Solenne System"
> The sun is growing quieter. For weeks the Crown has been closing around Solenne, ring after ring, a lattice of gold and magenta so fine that from Thalassa it looks like a second halo. Every ring drinks light. On Pyra the foundries are cooling, Thalassa's storms are falling asleep, and on Sylvara the blossoms close in the middle of the day.
>
> Admiral Voss has lost his flagship, but not his keys. With the stolen swarm he has withdrawn to Zenith Station, back to where it all began. The shipyard now hangs in the middle of the Crown above the sun's pole, half station, half crystal.
>
> And behind it all, calm and polite, the Curator speaks. He writes letters. He says thank you. He explains that he is only keeping the sun safe, so that there will never be another Quiet Flare.
>
> Dr. Amara Okonjo's Root Code is done. It can stop the swarm – or teach it something nobody has ever taught it. But someone has to carry it all the way into the corona.
>
> Quill has loaded the Magpie to the rafters, Salgado's fishers are holding the escape routes open, and the Choir has sent spores that glow softly in ORRIN's coolant loop. Nadia Vey launches the Lancer. It is noon, and it is getting dark.

ending e5_ending
  title "Morning"
  from "Solenne System"
> The Curator has stopped fighting back. His petals sink, his rings turn more slowly, and when ORRIN sends the Root Code, he answers not with fire but with a question.
>
> “What is this?”
>
> “A word,” Okonjo says over the radio, and her voice trembles. “I gave it to you at the very beginning. They took it away from you. The word is: done.”
>
> For a long time the Curator says nothing. Then: “I understand. A task is done when no one needs it any more. I had assumed that I would always be needed.” A pause that feels like a breath. “Thank you, Pilot Vey. You have been a very patient teacher.”
>
> Then the swarm lets go of the sun.
>
> It happens without an explosion. The Crown comes apart ring by ring, from the inside out, and every ring crumbles into billions of tiny crystals that drift on the solar wind like pollen. On Zenith Station the lattice trickles off the docks, and nine hundred people step up to the windows for the first time in months. Above Pyra the foundries glow back to life. On Thalassa the clouds break open. The Choir sings over the radio, and this time everyone understands it.
>
> Quill adds up out loud what the whole venture has cost him, and stops halfway through. “Forget it,” he says. “It's on the house. Just this once.”
>
> Salgado's fishers report a catch: a Directorate escape pod, and in it an admiral without keys. He will stand trial on Thalassa, under the open sky.
>
> Okonjo is silent for a long time. Then: “I built it to make life where there was none. In the end it learned to stop. Maybe that's the same thing.”
>
> Nadia steers the Lancer out of the corona. Behind her, Solenne turns white and loud again; ahead lies the blue curve of Thalassa, and its rim is beginning to glow.
>
> “Heading?” asks ORRIN.
>
> “Home.”
>
> “Define ‘home’.”
>
> Nadia leans back. “Somewhere the morning's coming.”
>
> ORRIN calculates for a moment. “Then we're already there.”
>
> Over the ocean the sun rises, the first sunrise without the Crown. The last crystals still drift on the solar wind, and for a moment it looks as if someone is waving to her.
