# Episode 2 "Tidefall" (Thalassa): intro, ending, briefings, radio lines, log crystals.

intro e2_intro
  title "Tidefall"
  from "Thalassa, 2614"
> Pyra lies behind you, a glowing dot on the rear monitor. Ahead, Thalassa grows: a world of water so blue that ORRIN checks three times whether the sensors are calibrated. No continents, only storm belts, polar ice and hundreds of floating cities drifting on the currents like water lilies made of steel.
>
> The Meridian Directorate has declared Thalassa a protectorate. Admiral Dacian Voss has parked his fleet in orbit and announces on every channel that no lattice will ever touch the cities. Yet night after night the swarm drifts into the harbours, and every time the protection ships arrive too late.
>
> Bastian Quill has customers down there, or so he claims. A fishing captain named Rui Salgado pays in tuna and favours, and he has sent out a distress call the Directorate apparently did not care to hear.
>
> The Magpie sinks through the clouds. Below her the Equatorial Sea glitters, and on the water something else glitters too: lattice, magenta and sea-glass green, growing where only yesterday there were fishing grounds.
>
> “Final approach in ninety seconds,” says ORRIN. “I suggest warming up the guns beforehand.”

ending e2_ending
  title "After the Flood"
  from "Corvina, Thalassa"
> The Leviathan breaks in two, and the lattice on its deck goes dark like a city in a blackout. The storm surge it drove before it rolls over Corvina's empty breakwaters and spends itself in the harbour basin. The city is still afloat.
>
> In the wreckage Salgado's crew finds the control relay. The carrier was not steered by the swarm but through it: someone gave it a course by way of the command keys. Someone with an admiral's rank.
>
> Admiral Voss was not aboard. His shuttle left orbit before the first shot was fired, bound for the Shatter, the asteroid belt. And the keys went with him.
>
> On the quay Rui Salgado presses a thermos into Nadia's hands. “Fish soup,” he says. “It's all we have. But we have a city again to cook it in.”
>
> Quill is already doing the sums. “The Shatter. Mining stations, derelict fleets, a nebula where you can't see your own hand in front of your face. I know someone there.” He always knows someone.
>
> ORRIN has gone through the Leviathan's control logs and found a name that keeps turning up: Dr. Amara Okonjo, the woman who wrote the Tessellate's code. Last known whereabouts: the Shatter.
>
> The Magpie lifts off. Below her the waves grow smooth.

  title "Surf Line"
  from "Bastian Quill"

Our client, Captain Salgado, fishes out on the surf line – or rather doesn't any more, because the swarm is doing the harvesting there now. Fly out and clean up. briefing e2_02_brief
  title "Corvina"
  from "Rui Salgado"
> Corvina is the oldest floating city in the Equatorial Sea, and for three weeks now the swarm has drifted into her harbours every single night. The Directorate says it's the current. I've sailed this current for thirty years, Pilot. It has never once pushed anyone into a harbour who didn't want to go there. Look where the lattice is flying.

briefing e2_03_brief
  title "Storm Belt"
  from "ORRIN"
> Salgado's people are stuck in a hideout beyond the storm belt. Wind speed at the front: a hundred and forty knots. The swarm taps the lightning for power, and its jellies ride the gusts. The direct route is the most dangerous one. It is also the only one on which the Directorate cannot watch us.

briefing e2_04_brief
  title "Dragnet"
  from "Rui Salgado"
> The swarm hauls its harvest along the sea lanes in convoys – skimmers, reefs, harvesters, everything the sea gives up. Last week my trawlers had something else in their nets: parts of buoys, stamped by the Directorate. Smash the convoys before they reach the reefs. And keep an eye on those buoys.

briefing e2_05_brief
  title "Tide Mill"
  from "Rui Salgado"
> The tide mill Nerissa used to power three cities. Now it sings. The swarm has turned its pressure pipes into organ pipes, and its song calls every lattice within two hundred kilometres to our harbour. Silence it, Pilot. Afterwards I'll show you what my radio operators recorded.

briefing e2_06_brief
  title "Orbital Anchor"
  from "Bastian Quill"
> Salgado's recordings leave no doubt: the Directorate's guide buoys transmit exactly the frequency the swarm follows – and so does the flagship. We fly up to the fleet anchorage and play them to Admiral Voss on an open channel, so everyone hears. Either he explains it, or he declares war on us. I've put money on the first. Not much.

briefing e2_07_brief
  title "Ice Edge"
  from "Rui Salgado"
> Voss has declared us pirates, and every city that takes us in along with us. My flotilla is heading north into the pack ice, where his cruisers can't follow. His fighters can. Keep them off our backs until the leads freeze shut behind us.

briefing e2_08_brief
  title "Marisol"
  from "ORRIN"
> Marisol, the city of a hundred markets, was abandoned by the Directorate ten days ago. Since then supply freighters have been landing there – not with relief goods, but with guide buoys. If we secure their manifests, we have proof Voss cannot talk his way out of. Bombers and lattice share the airspace there. Remarkably harmoniously.

briefing e2_09_brief
  title "Polar Night"
  from "ORRIN"
> Northwatch Station, the Directorate's polar base. Something big was fitted out here; the heat signature matches a carrier. Salgado's fishers watched the station from a distance for half a year, and their notes always end with the same word: keys. We should find out which ones.

briefing e2_10_brief
  title "Tidefall"
  from "Rui Salgado"
> The Leviathan has put to sea: a Directorate carrier overgrown with lattice, pushing a storm surge ahead of itself. Course: Corvina. With it fly Directorate fighters and Tessellate swarms, all in one formation. We're evacuating the city as fast as we can. You buy us the time.

briefing e2_s1_brief
  title "Drowned Archive"
  from "ORRIN"

briefing e2_s2_brief

  from "ORRIN"
